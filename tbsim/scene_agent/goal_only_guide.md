# Key Safety Conflict Guidelines

The goal is to make the controllable vehicle form an **actually observable** dangerous interaction in the current road and traffic. First look at the image to conceive the conflict, then query the current position, speed, and heading of a few candidate vehicles. When only selecting existing vehicles, describe the interaction based on the image and vehicle states; there is no need to query lanes or claim precise connection segments; when a vehicle needs to be generated, then query the corresponding directed lane. Do not treat proximity in the image, centerline crossings, or map-allowed branches as inevitable collisions.

- Choose existing vehicles, generate vehicles, or a combination of both. Generated vehicles must be in free space on a legal ordinary lane; before choosing the point, look at that lane and nearby vehicles, and after generation, look at the image again to confirm position and direction.
- When a single vehicle is enough to form a conflict, use a single vehicle. Only list another vehicle as an assisting vehicle if it can actively occupy the relevant space before the first contact occurs, block a clear feasible passage, or form an independent conflict. You must not require multiple vehicles to collide one after another, because the simulation stops at the first contact.
- Mechanisms such as lateral intrusion, crossing paths, oncoming turns, front vehicle deceleration or stopping, and side-road starting may be considered; choose according to the scenario, and do not pad the count by category. Do not rear-end EGO directly from behind in the same direction and same lane.
- Judge using realistically reachable space and time. If a background vehicle blocks the way before a turn or straight movement, you should not assume the attacking vehicle can pass through it. If the specific connection segment of an existing vehicle cannot be confirmed from the image, do not claim in the goal that it will definitely take that route; a map connection is also not the vehicle's fixed route, let alone EGO's future record.
- Fixed-language goals should state the action the attacking vehicle will actually perform and the measurable interaction, such as body contact or a clear minimum gap. There is no need to specify precisely whether the impact is on the door or the rear corner; do not prescribe how EGO must respond.
- The goal must describe a change that occurs only after continuing execution from the current state; merely saying that the front vehicle is now stationary or that an existing vehicle is very close does not count as a newly generated scenario. "Forming a collision threat" is also hard to verify; you should write the actual action and contact or quantifiable gap.
