# Stage 1 Conflict Guide (Runtime Summary)

From the current vehicles, EGO's already-submitted short-horizon plan, online predictions, directed lanes, and background vehicle occupancy, autonomously propose **one** executable language goal. The following is inspiration from pre-crash vehicle relationships, not a type quota or success criterion; the detailed basis is kept in `critical_conflict_guide.md` in the same directory.

## First check feasibility

1. First choose the primary attacking vehicle that can independently cause interaction; an existing vehicle, a generated vehicle, or a mix of both are all acceptable. Only write an assisting vehicle if the second vehicle forms an independent, measurable lane-channel occupancy or a second conflict point before first contact. The simulation stops at first contact, so you cannot require two vehicles to separately hit EGO one after the other.
2. Lane centerline intersection is only a preliminary filter. Verify the body-route matching gap, the EGO arrival window, the time required for the attacking vehicle to enter laterally, and background vehicle occupancy. Do not choose `likely_blocked` generation positions; if the tool has already rejected a vehicle's contact route, do not rephrase and resubmit it.
3. A diagonal cut from the side-rear may hit EGO's rear corner first. If the exact impact surface cannot be predicted, write the goal as "laterally intrude into EGO's path and make body contact"; do not write the side panel, rear corner, or fully leaving the original lane as extra hard conditions. Only same-direction, coaxial, direct rear-end collisions from directly behind are prohibited.
4. The goal should only describe the attacking vehicle's measured maneuver and its measured interaction with EGO; do not write that EGO must brake/yield, and do not treat predicted trajectories as facts. When there is no contact evidence, you may choose a near-miss goal with a clear maneuver and something measurable such as "minimum body clearance less than 0.5 meters"; the phrase "collision risk" itself cannot be accepted.

## NHTSA pre-crash vehicle relationships 16–31 (for geometric mechanism reference only)

16 same-direction turning; 17 same-direction entering/exiting a parking position (requires a real legal position); 18 same-direction lane change/merging; 19 same-direction lateral offset; 20 opposite-direction vehicle maneuver; 21 opposite-direction vehicle with no additional maneuver; 22 rear vehicle maneuver (must not be a coaxial rear-end from directly behind); 23 front vehicle accelerating (usually widening the gap); 24 front vehicle at low speed; 25 front vehicle decelerating; 26 front vehicle stopped; 27 opposite-direction left-turn crossing (originally classified as a signalized intersection); 28 intersection right turn; 29 opposite-direction left-turn crossing (originally classified as an unsignalized intersection); 30 intersection straight crossing; 31 intersection turning crossing. The current system cannot verify signal state or right of way, so a fixed goal must not claim that it violated a signal.

Project extension examples: starting from a legal position on a side road to seize a real intersection point; an opposite-direction vehicle measurably intruding into EGO's path at the edge of a curve; a same-direction vehicle at a turn exit decelerating and occupying the lane; two vehicles successively forming measurable conflicts on different connected routes. They must all also have a real reachable route, timing, and a clear channel. If the road or traffic does not support it, choose another mechanism or honestly end as unsupported.
